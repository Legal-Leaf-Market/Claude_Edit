# Reel captions for the Nicotia Market Instagram

Ready-to-paste captions for the reels on the Nicotia Market account. Three are
already live and moving without captions; this file could not see them
(Instagram is not reachable from the environment it was written in, the same
limitation the stompbox.world plan records), so it carries a caption for every
piece a Nicotia Market reel can have been cut from. Match each reel to its
entry by what is on screen; the "the reel this fits" line under each heading
says how to tell.

Everything here is written against the site itself and against
`public/js/share.js` in the NicotiaMarket repo, which is where the campaign's
share text lives. This file sits in this repo because this session pushes
here; long term it belongs beside `share.js`, either folded into the `PAGES`
payloads as a reel variant or as a docs file in that repo, so the two cannot
drift apart.

**Why caption a reel that is already taking off.** Reels are distributed on
the video, so a reel can run captionless; the caption is not what spreads it.
It is what a viewer who taps "more" reads, it is the only place the reel can
say where the full piece lives, it is where a claim gets its citation before
the comments demand one, and caption text is indexed by Instagram search where
on-video text is not reliably read. The reach is the video's. The caption is
what turns reach into readers.

**Adding a caption to a reel that is already posted:** open the reel, the
three-dot menu, Edit, paste, save. If a reel has a voiceover, also turn on
Instagram's auto-generated captions in the editor. Those on-video subtitles
are a different thing from this file, and the same instinct as filling alt
text on the grid posts.

## The rules every caption here follows

The same shape as the campaign text in `share.js`, and none of it is
decoration:

- **Every claim is on the page the caption links to.** Nothing here states a
  fact the site does not already carry. If a reel needs a claim the site
  lacks, the fix is the page first, then the caption.
- **The first line is the hook and stands alone.** Instagram folds a caption
  after roughly a line and a half; everything after "more" is opt-in, so the
  first sentence is a claim, not a greeting.
- **Every caption ends the same way: the page, then "Adults 21+ only.", then
  hashtags.** The 21+ line is the site's own standing rule and it reaches
  every caption, editorial or not.
- **No health claims, no cessation claims, no comparative-safety claims.**
  The site's Black Buffalo page states this rule for pages and it binds
  captions the same way. The one caption that promotes the shopping function
  carries the site's own warning line: nicotine is addictive, if you do not
  already use it, don't start.
- **No prices, except the one worked example the site itself keeps** (the
  30K against 60K per-puff arithmetic). Feed prices go stale, and a price in
  a caption outlives the price.
- **The library reels stay editorial.** History, language, scripture and
  poetry are the account's lane, and also the lane the platform leaves open: a
  reel that reads as a sales listing for nicotine products is the kind Meta's
  rules remove. Whether the FDA's warning-statement format for tobacco
  advertising reaches an aggregator's product posts is a question for a lawyer
  who has seen the setup, not something this file settles; the editorial
  pieces do not raise it, which is one more reason they are the lane.
- **No em dashes.** House rule on all the sister sites. Comma, colon or
  parentheses.

## 1. Nicotia: A Word for the People

- **Page:** nicotiamarket.com/story
- **The reel this fits:** the etymology story. Jean Nicot, the parcel to
  Paris, the queen's herb, the chain from tabaco to Nicotiana to Nicotia to
  nicotine. If the reel is the deep-time countdown (13.8 billion, a hundred
  million, 190) use entry 3 instead: both pages carry that ladder, but /story
  is the word and /older-than-the-word is the molecule.
- **If the comments ask for sources:** Jean Nicot's parcel to Catherine de
  Médicis, 1560; the genus fixed by Linnaeus, 1753; "Nicotia" first attested
  in the Oxford English Dictionary, 1830s.

**Caption**

```
A French ambassador posted some seeds in 1560 and ended up with his name on the molecule.

Jean Nicot did not discover tobacco. He did not grow it, and he was not the first European to send it anywhere. He was France's ambassador in Lisbon, he mailed seed and powdered leaf home to Catherine de Médicis, and the court was delighted. For years France called it the queen's herb. But names attach to whoever is holding the parcel when it lands.

Linnaeus made it permanent in 1753: the genus Nicotiana. The poets softened it to Nicotia in the 1830s. Chemistry borrowed nicotine from there.

The plant had a hundred names before Europe wrote one down. Ours is just the label that stuck. The whole four-hundred-year story:

Adults 21+ only.

#Nicotia #NicotiaMarket #etymology #JeanNicot #wordhistory #tobaccohistory
```

## 3. The Appetite Is Older Than the Word

- **Page:** nicotiamarket.com/older-than-the-word
- **The reel this fits:** the deep-time piece. The 13.8 billion / hundred
  million / 190 ladder, atoms forged in stars, the insecticide paradox, the
  Hagen argument.
- **If the comments ask for sources:** C10H14N2, Melsens 1843; Big Bang and
  stellar nucleosynthesis for the atoms; Sullivan, Hagen and Hammerstein,
  Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 2008.

**Caption**

```
The word is 190 years old. The molecule is about a hundred million. The atoms are 13.8 billion.

Nicotine is C10H14N2. The fourteen hydrogens were forged in the first minutes of the universe, and essentially none has been made since. The carbon and the nitrogen could not exist yet: they had to be cooked inside stars, and those stars had to die and throw the results outward, before there was an Earth for any of it to land on.

And the molecule itself is an insecticide, built in the root of a nightshade to punish whatever eats the leaf. Why anything evolved to seek that out is a real scientific puzzle (Sullivan, Hagen and Hammerstein put it to the field in 2008), and the mainstream answer quietly assumes a drug-free human past that may never have existed.

None of it makes anything safe. It is simply the accurate order of events.

We named a relationship. We did not invent one.

nicotiamarket.com/older-than-the-word

Adults 21+ only.

#Nicotia #NicotiaMarket #deeptime #evolution #anthropology #science
```

## 4. Nicotine at Work

- **Page:** nicotiamarket.com/nicotine-at-work
- **The reel this fits:** the smoke-break piece. Ashtrays on desks, 1964,
  Arizona 1973, hiring bans and surcharges, the break as the oldest labour
  protection on record.
- **If the comments ask for sources:** the US Surgeon General's 1964 report;
  Arizona's 1973 statute, the first comprehensive US state law; Exodus 31:17
  and Genesis 2:3 for the old end of it.

**Caption**

```
Offices used to come with ashtrays. On the desk. Standard issue.

For most of the twentieth century the workplace did not tolerate tobacco, it supplied it: military rations, Christmas hampers, the vending machine next to the coffee. Then 1964, and sixty years of policy aimed at smoke, the shared air, the haze. Arizona passed the first state law in 1973. Designated areas, then the property line, then hiring policies and premium surcharges.

Here is the thing: smoke and nicotine have now come apart, and most workplace rules are still pointing at something that is no longer there.

And the smoke break was never really about smoke. It was the only sanctioned way to stop working for five minutes without asking permission. One of the oldest pieces of labour protection anybody ever wrote down is a mandated break.

The whole history:

nicotiamarket.com/nicotine-at-work

Adults 21+ only.

#Nicotia #NicotiaMarket #smokebreak #workplace #laborhistory #HR
```

## 5. The Poets of Tobacco

- **Page:** nicotiamarket.com/poets-of-tobacco

  Abusers of Tabacco"; Rowlands, "In Praise of Tobacco"; Southey, "Elegy on a
  Quid of Tobacco", 1798; Lowell on Nicotia and the Muse.

**Caption**

```
In 1898 somebody published an entire anthology of tobacco poetry. Not a pamphlet. A book.

Lyra Nicotiana: four centuries of real poets writing love poems to one plant. William Barclay answering the plant's attackers in verse in 1614. Samuel Rowlands on the self-sufficiency of a man with "his kitchen in a box, and roast meat in a pipe." Robert Southey, a future Poet Laureate, writing an elegy for a used quid of tobacco in 1798, and it is genuinely good. James Russell Lowell went past praise and made Nicotia a goddess, "dearer to the Muse than all the grape's bewildering juice." From a man who knew exactly how much of the canon runs on wine.

Then, quite abruptly, it stopped. Nobody has really written one since.

The whole tradition, including our anthem, which is older than the United States:
```

## The six grid posts already have their captions

`PAGES` in `public/js/share.js` carries exactly six ready captions (the "Copy
the caption" button on the site): the home page, /word-for-smoke,
/older-than-the-word, /nicotine-at-work, /poets-of-tobacco and /library, with
/story covered by the default payload. If the six posts went up captionless
too, paste those, unchanged. The reason to not rewrite them is the reason
share.js gives for existing at all: the share text must be identical
everywhere or the campaign says two different things. The reel captions above
make the same claims in the same voice, tightened for a viewer who has just
watched the claim instead of read it.
